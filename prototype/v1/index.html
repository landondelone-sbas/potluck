    Product plan · working name Potluck

    A recipe finder that starts from what you already love.

    Most recipe apps start with a search box and a database. Potluck starts with you. On first launch you list the foods you like, sorted into five categories. After that, dinner is a mood: you drop favorites into a pot and the app hands back one dish, or a short list, built around them.

    Your food, not a databaseEvery ingredient in the pot comes from your own list. Nothing shows up that you don't like.
    A mood, not a queryYou choose by feel. A bit of this protein, that sauce. No typing, no filters to tune.
    One answer or a fewWhen the pot points at one dish, you get one. When it's a toss-up, you get up to three.

    How a session flows

    1. 1Set upFirst launch only. Walk the five categories, tap suggestions, add your own. Skip anything.
    2. 2Choose a moodThe pot and your favorites share one screen. Toggle a category, then drag favorites into the pot with your finger, or tap. Up to three per category.
    3. 3Cook it upThe pot simmers while the app scores dishes against what you chose.
    4. 4PlateOne winner or up to three. Each shows what it used, what the chef added, and what it left out.
    5. 5LearnMark what you made and rate it. Foods you try can join your pantry in one tap.

    Five categories, one pot

    ProteinThe star of the plate
    VegHalf the plate
    SpiceThe personality
    SauceThe finish
    StarchThe base: rice, pasta, potato, bread

    A category you leave empty is left to the chef. Skip the starch and the dish brings its own. That keeps the pot fast: you can drop in one thing or ten.

    How the pot decides

    • Every ingredient is tagged. "Chicken thighs" becomes chicken, "Hot honey" becomes hot and honey. Items you type get tagged by keyword. Unknown items still count, at lower weight.
    • Each dish lists which tags fit each category. A fit scores highest. A spice or sauce that suits the dish's cuisine scores less. Anything else is left out, and the card says so.
    • Signature items give a bonus: tortillas pull toward tacos, pasta toward a pasta skillet.
    • If the top dish clearly beats the rest, you get one. If several land within about 10 percent of it, you get up to three.
    • The wildcard card is where AI takes over later.

    Eating well, built in

    • A plate nudge under the pot. No veg yet? It says so, since about half the plate as vegetables is a common guideline.
    • Every dish carries one lighter swap, like brown rice or extra veg.
    • Set up can ask about diet and allergies. These act as hard filters, never gentle hints.
    • Later: calories and macros per serving, and a weekly balance view.

    General guidance only, not medical or dietetic advice.

    Where AI comes in

    PrototypeRulesTags and a hand-written dish library. Fast, predictable, free to run, and enough to test whether the pot feels good.
    NextAI chefSend the pot, the pantry, diet and time available to an AI model. Get back a dish name, amounts and steps in a fixed format. It starts as the wildcard card.
    LaterAI that knows youLearns from ratings, suggests new favorites you'd probably like, builds a week of pots and a grocery list.

    Guardrails: check allergens in code against the Set up answers, never only in the prompt. Keep the rules engine as the offline fallback.

    Favorite { id, name, category, tags[], emoji }
Pot      { items: Favorite.id[]   // max 3 per category, 12 total }
Dish     { id, cuisine, titleTemplate,
           wants { category: tags[] },
           signature[], defaults, steps[], tip }
Result   { dish, score, used[], chefAdds[], leftOut[] }

    Decisions for you

    1. Veg and starch. Decided. Two separate categories, Veg and Starch, as in this prototype.
    2. Pot size. One pot per meal, or one per person at the table?
    3. Staples. Should oil, salt and pepper always be assumed, so they never need a pick?
    4. Dish source. A hand-written library first, licensed recipe data, or AI-written dishes from day one?
    5. Platform. A phone-first web app to start, or a native app?
